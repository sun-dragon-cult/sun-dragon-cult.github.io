![Download Count](https://img.shields.io/github/downloads/sun-dragon-cult/fvtt-system-rqg/v6.1.0/rqg.zip)

This release's headline feature is a real magic point source picker: casting a spell now lets you
choose where the points come from whenever more than one source is available. POW-storing crystals
work as an alternate source instead of the old max-boosting hack, a Rune Priest's Allied Spirit can
share magic points, Rune points, and spell knowledge with its priest, spirits trapped in an item via
a Binding Enchantment can be drawn on the same way, and a Spell Matrix Enchantment lets a Spirit
Magic spell travel with a physical item for anyone holding it to cast.

Magic Points and natural Hit Point healing also stop being something you have to track by hand: both
now recover automatically as game time passes, computed straight from the world clock instead of a
manual catch-up step. And a quadruped creature like a horse gets its own hit-location layout on the
Combat tab instead of a default table layout.

Alongside that, resolving a season's worth of pending Experience checks no longer means chasing
across the Skills, Rune, and Passion tabs to find every one and opening a separate dialog for each.
A new Experience Roll Session lists everything pending at once with an optional Roll All action.

## Magic Point Source Picker

<GithubIssue issue="956" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1023" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Any Gear, Weapon, or Armor item can now act as a POW-storing crystal. Once a GM sets a max stored
points value on the item, casting a Spirit or Rune Magic spell lets the caster choose where the
points are drawn from: their own pool, a storage item, an Allied Spirit bond, or "Auto," which
drains through all of them in a single player-defined order - the caster's own pool is just one
entry in that order, not a special fallback after it. That order - and which items show up as
sources at all - is managed from a new Magic Point Sources popout on the character sheet, with
drag-and-drop reordering. That popout also has a button to move Magic Points from the actor into
the crystal, moving as many as it can without knocking the actor unconscious.

{/* screenshot: Magic Point Sources popout, including the move-to-crystal button */}

Every option in the picker's dropdown - other than "Auto" itself, since it isn't a single pool -
shows its current/max Magic Points directly, so you can tell at a glance which source actually has
anything left before choosing.

{/* screenshot: source picker in the Cast Spirit Magic dialog */}

This replaces the old approach of bumping an actor's Magic Point max via an Active Effect on the
item, which merged a crystal's points into the owner's own pool instead of keeping them as a
separate, spendable reserve. Any item still using the old approach is migrated automatically the
next time your world runs its migrations - no manual cleanup needed.

:::caution Manual cleanup needed if you tracked crystal charges via Quantity

If you instead house-ruled a crystal's remaining points by hand using its Quantity field (e.g.
Quantity 5 meaning 5 points left, decrementing it as points were spent), that pattern isn't covered
by the automatic migration - there's nothing in the data for it to recognize. Set the item's new
Stored Magic Points value/max yourself, then set Quantity back to 1 - and consider switching the
item's type to Unique if you no longer need it to behave as a Consumable.

:::

## Allied Spirits

<GithubIssue issue="957" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1002" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

A Rune Priest's Allied Spirit (Core p.277) is now more than flavor text. Link the bond partner from
the priest's Combat tab in edit mode by dragging the spirit actor to the Allied Spirit dropzone, and
from then on - in either direction - the pair share Magic Points, Rune Points for any cult they're
both initiated to, and known Spirit Magic and Rune Magic spells. That means a priest can cast a
spell their Allied Spirit knows (or vice versa) by drawing on the other's points, even for spells
they never personally learned - shown on the caster's own Spirit/Rune Magic tab under a "From
\{name\}:" divider so it's clear the knowledge isn't really theirs.

{/* screenshot: Allied Spirit link on the Combat tab */}

{/* screenshot: shared spells on the Rune Magic tab */}

:::info

Rune Point sharing matches the priest's and Allied Spirit's cults by their Rqid - something cults
never needed before this release, so an older cult item in your world may not have one. The Rune
Magic casting dialog now tells you explicitly when a bonded Allied Spirit exists but isn't
recognized as sharing a cult, instead of the source picker just silently not appearing, and the Add
Rqids dialog that pops up during migrations now includes cults that lack Rqids, so it can fix this
for you.

:::

## Bound Spirits

<GithubIssue issue="999" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

A spirit trapped in an item via a Binding Enchantment (Core p.249) can now be linked from that
item's sheet by dragging the spirit's actor onto it. Once bound, whoever holds the item can draw on
the spirit's Magic Points during casting - and can cast any Spirit Magic spell the spirit itself
knows, shown under its own "From \{spirit\} in \{item\}:" divider.

{/* screenshot: Bound Spirit dropzone on an item sheet */}

{/* screenshot: Spirit Magic tab showing a "From {spirit} in {item}:" entry */}

Draining a bound spirit's Magic Points to zero releases it from the binding, and a cast that would
do that prompts for confirmation first. The cap of CHA÷3 total bound spirits is shown as an
informational count on the Combat tab, with a warning if you go over it - since too many bound
spirits should provoke a rebellion.

## Spell Matrix Enchantments

<GithubIssue issue="959" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1021" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Any Gear, Weapon, or Armor item can now carry a Spell Matrix Enchantment: drag a Spirit Magic spell
onto the item's sheet to enchant it, and from then on whoever is holding the item can cast that
spell, whether or not they personally know it.

{/* screenshot: Spell Matrix dropzone for a Spirit Magic spell on an item sheet */}

Casting it works straight from the actor sheet - a gear-row indicator for a quick roll, or its own
entry on the Spirit Magic tab under a "Matrix:" divider, alongside the existing "Allied Spirit:" and
"Bound Spirit:" dividers.

{/* screenshot: Spirit Magic tab showing a "Matrix:" entry */}

Casting a matrix spell uses the caster's own POW×5 chance and their own Magic Points, drawn through
the same source picker as any other spell. It doesn't count against the holder's own CHA
spell-point limit, since it was never one of their personally-learned spells.

An item isn't limited to one enchanted spell either: drop additional Spirit Magic spells onto the
same item and each gets its own chip on the item sheet, with its own GM-only points field and its
own delete button (points are only editable when the underlying spell is variable - a fixed-level
spell shows its points read-only). The gear-row badge stays a one-click cast when the item carries
exactly one matrix spell; with two or more it becomes an info-only badge whose tooltip lists them
all, since a single click can no longer pick one for you - casting them then happens from their own
entries on the Spirit Magic tab instead. The point of that is that you could, for example, have
several matrix-enchanted spells embedded in a single sword.

:::caution The old "Held in Matrix" checkbox on a personal spell is now deprecated

Before this release, the closest thing to a matrix was the "Held in Matrix" checkbox on a
character's own Spirit Magic spell (`isMatrix`) - it only ever excluded that spell from the caster's
own CHA spell-point limit, with no physical item, no sharing with anyone else, and no connection to
this feature's data. GMs ended up overloading that one checkbox for two different situations: a real
physical matrix, or a bound/allied spirit's spell copied onto the character just so it wouldn't
count against their CHA limit - a case this release's Allied Spirit and Bound Spirit spell-sharing
now handles properly instead. The checkbox itself still exists and still works exactly as before,
but it's superseded and should be treated as deprecated.

There's no fully automatic migration for it, and can't reasonably be one: nothing in the data says
which of the two situations above an `isMatrix` spell represents, and for the real-matrix case,
nothing says which physical item (if any) it was meant to live in - both are always a GM call. To
make it easier to find and convert the spells that have this isMatrix set, there's a "Migrate
isMatrix Spells" macro in the "System Macros" compendium, in the "RuneQuest Glorantha System/Macros"
folder. It lists every `isMatrix` spell in the world - with the actor and spell as clickable links,
and the spell's Focus text shown, since many GMs use that field to note which item a matrix should
live in - and lets you handle each one at your own pace: "Create Gear" creates a new Gear item on
the same actor with the Spell Matrix Enchantment already linked at the same points and opens it for
you to rename, and "Delete Spirit Magic" (with a confirmation prompt) removes the now-obsolete
original Spirit Magic item once you're happy with its replacement, or once you've set up the bonded
spirit's proper spell-sharing instead. Neither action fires until you choose it, and a row you
resolve just stops showing up the next time the macro runs.

:::

:::info Not an embedded copy of the spell

Enchanting a matrix only stores a link to the spell (a Rqid) plus the number of points enchanted
into it - the spell's own mechanics are never embedded on the item or copied onto it. Every time the
matrix is cast, the system resolves that Rqid fresh against whatever world or compendium item
currently matches it, so a later correction to the spell (or an update to the compendium it came
from) is picked up automatically instead of the matrix carrying a stale copy. It's a new way for an
item to grant a spell in this system - the same Rqid lookup that already backs cross-references
elsewhere, applied here to resolve a spell's mechanics live rather than storing them.

:::

## Magic Point Recovery

<GithubIssue issue="512" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1028" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Magic Points now recover on their own as game time passes, computed straight from Foundry's world
clock. Advancing that clock is a calendar module's job (e.g. Simple Calendar) - Foundry itself has
no built-in way to move it forward, so without one there's no game time for this to catch up
against. Simply opening a character's sheet is enough to catch it up, and so is the world clock
advancing while the sheet is already open. There is a new recovery-rate indicator next to Magic
Points on the character header that shows the resulting points/day and time-per-point on hover.

{/* screenshot: Magic Point recovery-rate tooltip on the character header */}

A gift or HeroQuest effect that speeds up or slows down recovery can target the new
`system.attributes.magicPointRecoveryRateFactor` field directly with an Active Effect - e.g., a
Humakti gift doubling recovery speed could `OVERRIDE` this to 2, in effect halving the time it takes
to recover a Magic Point.

## Natural Healing

<GithubIssue issue="436" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1031" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Natural Hit Point healing now works the same way as Magic Point recovery: it's computed from elapsed
game time instead of anything applied by hand. Each wounded hit location recovers its own healing
rate in points, applied as a lump at the end of each elapsed game week rather than prorated,
matching the Core rule. A location with more than one wound splits the recovered points evenly
across them, with any remainder going to the lightest wound, and a severed or useless location's hit
points still recover naturally even though regaining the use of the limb still takes magic capable
of regrowing it.

{/* OPEN QUESTION: how should "resting" be handled/enforced for natural healing - not yet decided,
    see conversation with wake before publishing */}

Whether a character was actually resting - which Core requires for natural healing to apply - isn't
tracked or enforced; that stays a GM call, the same as everywhere else this release leaves a narrow
RAW condition to the table rather than hard-coding it.

## Quadruped Hit-Location Visualization

<GithubIssue issue="1033" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

The hit-location visualization on the Combat tab - previously laid out for humanoid actors only -
now recognizes quadruped creatures too, based on their hit locations, and shows them against their
own silhouette (head, forequarters, hindquarters, legs) instead of a default table layout.

{/* screenshot: horse hit locations on the Combat tab */}

## Experience Roll Session

<GithubIssue issue="912" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

A new menu item on the actor sheet header opens a single-screen session listing every pending
Experience check on the character at once - skills, Runes, Passions, and POW - with an optional Roll
All action and a fixed/random gain toggle, instead of working through each one's own Improve dialog
in turn.

{/* screenshot: Experience Roll Session dialog */}

Rolling through the per-item Improve dialogs (for Training and Research, which stay there) and the
new session both now go through the same shared resolver, so a gain roll behaves identically either
way - including a fix along the way for negative gain rolls incorrectly shrinking a value, and for
Rune/Passion improvements not respecting their 100% cap.
