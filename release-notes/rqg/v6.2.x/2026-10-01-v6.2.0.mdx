This release's headline feature is spell effects. Cast a spell such as Bladesharp, Protection or
Strength, and when it takes effect, the target's owner clicks Apply on the chat card to put the
spell's effect on the right actor or weapon, with the right level and duration. Casting the same
spell again, or a spell that can't share a target with one already there, is handled for you, and
every spell on a token shows at the top of the token HUD, where the GM can open, remove or add one.

It builds on resisted spells: a spell like Sleep or Befuddle now hands the target's owner a card to
resist or accept, and rolls POW against POW on the resistance table.

Active Effects get more capable too: an effect on an actor can now change the actor's items, such as
one skill, every hit location, or the weapon it's on.

Alongside that, a new actor can start from a template actor, and many more things can be dragged to
the hotbar.

## Spell Effects

<GithubIssue issue="443" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1079" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

### Casting and applying

<GithubIssue issue="1078" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

The spell card now shows one row per target, saying whether the spell took effect. If the outcome
needs a judgment call, the GM can rule on each row: takes effect, or no effect. When the spell took
effect, the target's owner gets an **Apply** button. Clicking it puts the spell's effect on the
target:

- A spell that works on a weapon, like Bladesharp or Fireblade, goes on the target's equipped weapon
  it fits, and asks which one if there's more than one.
- Any other spell goes on the target itself.
- The effect is named after the spell and its level, like "Bladesharp (3)", and lasts the spell's
  duration: 2 minutes for spirit magic, 15 minutes for rune magic. A few effects have a longer
  duration of their own, like Catseye's 12 hours.

### Which spells have effects

The spell effects ship with wiki module 2.1.0, which has an effect for every spirit and rune spell
that lasts for a while. Some change the sheet, like Strength raising STR or Bladesharp adding to the
weapon's attack and damage. The others are reminders that show the spell is active until it runs
out. See the [Wiki 2.1.0 release notes](https://sun-dragon-cult.github.io/release-notes/wiki-v2.1.0)
for the full list.

:::warning Update the wiki module first

Update the RQG wiki module to 2.1.0 **before** the system. When the world is upgraded, the migration
links the spells already on your actors to their effects by looking them up in the wiki module's
compendiums. If the wiki module is still the old version, those spells get no effect link. If that
happens, update the wiki module and run the migration again: as the GM, click Trigger World
Migration in the Game Settings sidebar tab. The world reloads and migrates again, and this time the
spells get their links. Links that are already there are left alone, so running it again is safe.

:::

### Recasting and incompatible spells

By the rules, casting a spell on a target that already has the same spell only works if the new
casting is stronger, and some spells can't be on the same target at once. For example, Bladesharp
and Fireblade can't share a weapon, and neither can Protection and Shimmer on a person. When a spell
has no effect for one of these reasons, Apply tells you why and the card records it. Only the
target's owner sees the reason.

The world setting **Recast Spells Take Effect** chooses the rule: **Only if stronger** (the rules as
written) or **Always**, where every new casting replaces the old one.

### Berserker and Fanaticism

<GithubIssue issue="1170" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Berserker and Fanaticism now change the rolls of whoever has them, and the roll dialogs show it:

- **Berserker:** attacks get half again, or double against a creature with the Chaos rune. Parrying,
  dodging and casting aren't allowed: the dialog says why and can't roll. CON is increased by half,
  CON rolls succeed at 95%, and the berserker has Countermagic 2, or 4 against spells from chaotic
  casters. When the spell ends, the berserker becomes Helpless. Berserker replaces a Fanaticism on
  the target.
- **Fanaticism:** attacks get half again, parrying isn't allowed and Dodge is halved. Which spells a
  fanatic may still cast is up to the GM.

Being preserved from shock and unconsciousness while berserk is also up to the GM. The harpy in the
wiki module now has the Chaos rune.

### Spell effects on the token

A token's spell effects are shown at the top of the token HUD's status palette, above the condition
markers, with their name, the item they're on and the time left. The GM can click one to open it,
right-click to remove it, or change its level on the effect's sheet.

Hovering over an effect also says what it does. **Has automated effects** means it changes the sheet
or its spell's rules act while it is on, like Bladesharp or Warding. **Only marks the spell while it
lasts** means the GM rules on what it does, like Befuddle. A partly automated effect adds what is
left to the GM, for example "Not automated: −1 strike rank" on Mobility.

When a spell runs out, its effect is removed from the token and its item.

The GM can also add a spell effect without casting, for example for a spell cast before the scene
started. Click the **+** in the same section, search for the spell, set its level, and click Add.

### Token status markers

Because spells now show on tokens as spell effects, the token HUD's status palette no longer holds
spell markers. Protection, Strength, Bladesharp and the other spell statuses are gone, and so are
Ironhand, Parry and the 1–6 counters. The counters were a way to note a spell's level, which the
spell effect's name now shows.

The palette now has general condition markers only. The new ones are Helpless, Concentrating, In
Spirit Combat, Discorporate, Possessed, Invisible, Flying and Burning, and Restrained is now called
Immobilized.

:::note Upgrading

A spell status that is already on a token when you upgrade stays on it as an ordinary effect until
it runs out. You can also delete it from the actor's Effects tab. For a spell that has no spell
effect yet, add a plain effect on the actor's Effects tab.

:::

## Healing

<GithubIssue issue="1171" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

The heal button on a hit location now opens one dialog for the whole actor. It lists every wounded
location with its hit points and wounds, with the clicked location's largest wound selected. Heal
one wound by up to the chosen points, the way First Aid treats one injury at a time, or switch to
**Remove All Damage** to clear every location at once.

A severed limb is no longer reattached just because 6 or more points were healed on it. Tick
**Reattach** next to its wounds instead, since whether all parts are at hand is the GM's call.

Heal, Heal Wound and Heal Body now heal when you click Apply on their cast card:

- **Heal** puts its points on one location. Click a wound to heal it first; the rest of the points
  go to the location's other wounds. With 6 points it can reattach a severed limb.
- **Heal Wound** heals as many hit points as the magic points spent with it. Click the dots under
  each wound to choose where the healing goes. With 6 magic points it can reattach one severed limb.
- **Heal Body** heals every location and the total hit points, and reattaches severed limbs.

First Aid works the same way. Target the wounded token, or no one to treat yourself, and roll First
Aid. On a success the roll card gets an Apply button: it asks which wound to treat, then rolls the
healing (1D3, 2D3 on a special, 1D3+3 on a critical) for that wound. On a fumble Apply asks which
location was treated and does 1D3 damage to it instead. Stopping bleeding and treating each injury
only once are still up to the GM. This needs the wiki module.

## Active Effects That Change an Actor's Items

<GithubIssue issue="920" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Active Effects that change a value on the actor's items have a new, more versatile key format.
Before, such a change had to use the **Custom** change type and could only add to the value. Now it
can use all of Foundry's change types. For example, it can raise one skill, add armor to every hit
location, or improve the weapon it's on. Start the change's key with `@`, followed by which items to
change, a `:`, and the path of the value:

| Key                       | Changes                                              | Example                                                 |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `@<rqid>:system.<path>`   | The actor's item with that rqid                      | `@i.skill.dodge:system.baseChance`, add 10              |
| `@~<regex>:system.<path>` | Every item whose rqid matches the regular expression | `@~^i\.hit-location\.:system.naturalAp`, add 2          |
| `@.:system.<path>`        | The item the effect is on                            | `@.:system.effect.add.melee.attack` on a weapon, add 10 |

- **Change types:** these changes use Foundry's normal types, Add, Multiply, Override, Upgrade and
  Downgrade, just like any other key. Keys that don't start with `@` work exactly as they do in
  Foundry.
- **Weapon bonuses:** the `system.effect.add` values are bonuses added on top of a value. A weapon
  has them for melee and missile attack, parry and damage. The damage bonus is new in this release,
  and a weapon's rolled damage never goes below 0.
- **Spell level:** in an effect that comes from a spell, `@level` in the value is the level the
  spell was cast at. For example, Bladesharp adds `@level * 5` to attack.
- **Warnings:** a key that can't be used, such as a mistyped rqid, an rqid the actor has no item
  for, or a change type a bonus can't use, shows the GM a warning saying what's wrong. When the
  world loads, these are collected into one summary.

See [Rqid](/rqg-system/api/rqid) for the bonus values you can change and more examples.

:::warning Changed keys

Before this release, a key like `i.skill.dodge:system.baseChance` on the **Custom** change type
changed an item. The world migration rewrites these to the new form,
`@i.skill.dodge:system.baseChance` on **Add**, so they keep doing the same thing. A key in the old
form that the migration can't reach, such as one typed afterwards or in a module's compendium, shows
the GM a warning and does nothing until it's updated.

:::

## Spell Areas on the Scene

<GithubIssue issue="1168" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Spells that affect a place, like Shake Earth, Warding or Sanctify, now make a scene Region. The
caster places it, snapped to the scene's grid, and the squares or hexes it will cover light up as it
moves:

- **Ranged spells** must lie wholly within range, centered on a point the caster can see. The scene
  is dimmed outside what the caster's token can see within range, and the area can't be placed
  there. For a point the caster perceives by magic, the GM places it in sight and then drags the
  Region where it belongs.
- **Rituals** can be placed anywhere, as a ritual is held where its area is. A ward is placed by
  clicking its four wands.

The GM and the caster's players see the area; a spell like Mist Cloud is seen by everyone. The area
ends when the spell does, and the GM can move or delete it like any Region. With the wiki module,
Shake Earth hinders everyone inside, wards strike hostile intruders and stop spells cast from
outside, and Sanctify tells the caster when a non-initiate or a spell crosses into it.

:::info

Spell areas measure true distance in meters, as the rules do. On square grids, Foundry's default
counts a diagonal step as one space, so the movement ruler lets tokens go about 40% farther
diagonally than the rules allow. Set **Configure Settings → Core → Grid Diagonals** to **Exact** to
make the ruler and the spell areas agree. Hex and gridless scenes aren't affected. See
[Setting Up Scenes](/rqg-system/getting-started/scenes).

:::

## Resisted Spells and Resistance Rolls

<GithubIssue issue="757" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="758" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1066" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1076" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1077" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

A spell that the target can resist, like Sleep, Befuddle or Slow, now checks its target before any
magic points are spent. The cast dialog picks the target from the tokens you can see, nearest first,
or yourself. Cast with no target, the card waits for the GM to rule on it. The cast then posts a
single card to the target's owner, who chooses **Resist** or **Accept**. The resistance roll pits
the caster's POW against the target's POW on the resistance table. The target doesn't see which
spell it is until it takes effect.

Before anyone resists, each target's Countermagic, Shield or ward meets the spell, and a spell they
stop has no effect on that target and asks for no roll. This works for every spell, automated or
not.

Some spells resist differently:

- **One roll per target.** Turn Undead and Teleportation can be cast at several targets, and each
  target resists or accepts on its own card. Undead have no POW, so Turn Undead is resisted with the
  target's current magic points, and it reaches one undead per point.
- **Spirit combat.** Bind Ghost and Resurrect only work if the caster wins spirit combat. Until
  spirit combat is automated, the cast card waits for the GM to rule whether the caster won.
- **Area spells.** Harmony and Inviolable make one resistance roll for everyone within their radius,
  the caster included, and the card says what it overcomes, for example "affects POW 15 or lower".
  Inviolable's own text reads the resistance table the other way round; it uses the same reading as
  Harmony and every other resistance roll. The cast dialog lights up the area and targets everyone
  in it for you.
- **Reflection.** A spell that fails against a target protected by Reflection is sent back at its
  caster. The caster's own Countermagic or Shield meets it first, then the caster resists it with
  their own POW. A spell is only reflected once.
  <GithubIssue issue="1178" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

The GM can also send any resistance roll to a player, for example STR against a trap, from the actor
sheet's Combat tab, the combat tracker, the Actors sidebar, or a Token HUD button. The Token HUD
button can be turned off in the settings.

## Start a New Actor from a Template

<GithubIssue issue="636" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="778" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1055" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

Foundry's **Create Actor** dialog has a new **Start from** choice. **Blank** works as before, and
**Choose template...** opens a list of template actors from the world and the compendiums. The wiki
module provides templates for adventurers, like "Human (Base)" and "Mostali (Dwarf)", and for
creatures, like animals, chaos monsters, elementals, mounts and spirits. Search the list by name, or
filter it by class, role and species. The new actor is a copy of the template, with all its items.

See [Creating an Adventurer](/rqg-system/getting-started/creating-actor) for how to make your own
actors show up as templates.

## API for Macros and Modules

<GithubIssue issue="1129" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
<GithubIssue issue="1131" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

The system now has a documented API, `game.system.api`, for macros and modules. A macro can roll
skills, runes, passions and characteristics, attack with a weapon, and cast spirit and rune magic,
with the roll dialog or without it. It can also read a character's skills, weapon attacks and the
spells they can cast, including spells from an Allied Spirit, a bound spirit or a spell matrix.
Values stored on a character, like hit points and magic points, are read straight from the character
data, which is now also part of the API.

See the [API overview](/rqg-system/api) to get started, and the
[macro examples](/rqg-system/api/macro-examples) for ready-to-use macros. From now on, changes that
can break macros or modules are listed in the release notes.

### Changes that can break macros

- `actor.applyDamage(...)` is gone. Use `actor.damage({ amount, location, armorPoints })` to damage
  a hit location, where `location` is a hit location item or `"random"` and `armorPoints` (default
  the location's own armor) is subtracted first, and
  `actor.heal({ location, points, wound, restoreSevered })` to heal one. Both return what they did.
- `item.applyWound(...)` and `item.healWound(...)` on hit location items are gone too; use
  `actor.damage` and `actor.heal`.

<GithubIssue issue="1171" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />

### Roll methods on actors and items

Rolls now have one method each, taking an options object:

- `item.roll(options)` rolls a skill, rune or passion.
- `actor.rollCharacteristic(name, options)` rolls a characteristic.
- `actor.rollReputation(options)` rolls Reputation.
- `item.cast(options)` casts a spirit or rune magic spell as its owner, and
  `actor.castSpell(spell, options)` casts one with that actor as the caster, including spells from
  an Allied Spirit, a bound spirit or a matrix.
- `item.attack({ usage })` opens the attack dialog for a weapon, with `usage` preselected.

Without `skipDialog: true` they open the roll dialog. With it they roll at once and return the roll.

Modules can change and react to rolls with hooks:

| Prepare the roll                | Before the roll             | After the roll           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `rqg.prepareAbilityRoll`        | `rqg.preAbilityRoll`        | `rqg.abilityRoll`        |
| `rqg.prepareCharacteristicRoll` | `rqg.preCharacteristicRoll` | `rqg.characteristicRoll` |
| `rqg.prepareReputationRoll`     | `rqg.preReputationRoll`     | `rqg.reputationRoll`     |
| `rqg.prepareSpellCast`          | `rqg.preSpellCast`          | `rqg.spellCast`          |
| `rqg.prepareAttack`             | `rqg.preAttack`             | `rqg.attack`             |
| `rqg.prepareDefence`            | `rqg.preDefence`            | `rqg.defence`            |

A "prepare" hook adds modifiers to the roll, or says why the roll isn't allowed, for example a
berserker's parry. The roll dialog shows both before Roll is clicked, so the chance in the dialog is
the chance that's rolled. A "pre" hook runs once, when the roll is made, and can cancel it. See
[Roll hooks](/rqg-system/api/hooks).

Modules can also make a skill do something after it's rolled, the way the wiki module's First Aid
heals a wound, and a spell's effect can change rolls through roll entries, like Berserker and
Fanaticism. A spell's behavior can also make a spell area on the scene, limit how many targets the
spell reaches, and roll its damage and hit location like an attack. See
[Behaviors](/rqg-system/api/behaviors).

The old methods still work until RQG 6.4.0, but log a warning in the browser console that names the
replacement:

| Old method                                                       | Replacement                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `item.abilityRoll` / `item.abilityRollImmediate`                 | `item.roll`                |
| `actor.characteristicRoll` / `actor.characteristicRollImmediate` | `actor.rollCharacteristic` |
| `actor.reputationRoll` / `actor.reputationRollImmediate`         | `actor.rollReputation`     |
| `item.spiritMagicRoll` / `item.spiritMagicRollImmediate`         | `item.cast`                |
| `item.runeMagicRoll` / `item.runeMagicRollImmediate`             | `item.cast`                |

Hotbar macros made by dragging a skill to the hotbar in earlier versions use the old method. To
update one, drag the skill to the hotbar again.

## Old Sheets Will Be Removed in 6.3.0

The newer character and item sheets are the default. Their names end in **V2**, like "Character
Sheet V2". The old sheets, like "Character Sheet" and "Passion Item Sheet", can still be picked in a
document's **Sheet** configuration, but they will be removed in RQG 6.3.0. Actors and items set to
an old sheet then open in the V2 sheet automatically, so there's nothing you need to do. Until then,
opening an old sheet logs a warning in the browser console.

## For Content Creators

- Spells link to their effect with an **Effect** field on the spell sheet, next to the description
  link. Spells already on actors get their link from the compendium during the world migration.
  <GithubIssue issue="1086" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
- Spells have a **Resisted By** field that says how a target can resist them: a resistance roll
  against one target or each target, spirit combat, or nothing. A **Resisted With** field says what
  the target resists with: POW, or current magic points.
  <GithubIssue issue="1068" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
- Spells record what they can be cast on and what kind of effect they have, in two new fields.
  <GithubIssue issue="1080" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />
- Errors and warnings now carry a code like `RQG-W-0012`, which makes them easier to report and look
  up.
