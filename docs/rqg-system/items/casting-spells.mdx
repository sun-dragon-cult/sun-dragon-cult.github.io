This page follows a spell from the cast dialog to the effect on its target, in the order the system
handles it. Spirit magic and Rune magic go the same way. What the spell then does is covered on
[Spells](/rqg-system/items/spells).

## 1. The cast dialog

The dialog shows the caster, the targets, the level and any magic points to boost the spell with.
The level is at least 1 and a boost at least 0.

- **Targets** are the tokens you have targeted when you press Cast.
- **Spells centered on the caster**, like Harmony and Inviolable, take everyone within their radius
  instead, the caster included, whatever is targeted. The dialog says how far that is, and the
  squares or hexes it covers are lit up on the scene, growing and shrinking with the level. Tokens
  hidden from the caster's player aren't included; the GM handles those.
- **Spells resisted by a single target** take one target, picked in the dialog's dropdown: the
  tokens you can see, nearest first, or "(yourself)".
- **With nothing targeted**, a spell that can't be resisted (like Protection), or one that can only
  be on its caster or their weapon (like Bladesharp), goes on the caster. A spell that can be
  resisted gets a pending "No target" row for the GM to rule on.
- **Some spells reach a limited number of targets**, like Turn Undead, one undead per point. The
  dialog says how many, and casting at more is refused.

## 2. The cast roll

The cast card shows the roll and the points spent. If the cast fails, no target is affected and
nothing else happens.

A spell's **strength** against defenses is its points: magic points as they are, Rune points double,
plus any boost. A 2-point Rune spell boosted with 1 magic point has strength 5.

## 3. Spell areas on the way

A spell cast from outside a spell area at a target inside it crosses the area's boundary, and the
area can react, once per cast:

- A **ward** (Warding or Create Market) sounds its alarm, heard by the GM and the players with
  tokens within 500 m.
- **Sanctify** alerts its caster that a spell crossed in. The caster's players aren't told whose;
  the GM is.

This happens whether the spell then gets through or not.

## 4. Protective spells

Before anyone resists, each target's protective spells meet the spell, in layers from the outside
in. The first one that stops it settles that target: its row on the card reads "No effect", stopped
by a protective spell, and it gets no resistance roll. The card doesn't say which spell stopped it.

| Layer            | Stops the spell when its strength is…       | Used up?                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ward**         | at most the ward's Countermagic + 1         | Never. A spell it stops never reaches the target's own defenses.            |
| **Countermagic** | 2 or more below it, or within 1 point of it | Within 1 point, both are gone. A spell 2 or more points stronger breaks it. |
| **Shield**       | at most twice its points                    | Never.                                                                      |

A ward only protects non-hostile tokens inside it, against spells from outside it. Berserker counts
as Countermagic 2, or 4 against a chaotic caster. A target's own defenses don't stop a spell it
casts on itself, and spells marked **Gets through Countermagic and Shield**, like Dismiss Magic,
pass them all.

This works for every spell, including ones with no automation at all.

## 5. Resistance

What happens next depends on the spell's **Resisted By** field:

| Resisted By                         | What happens                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **None**                            | The spell takes effect on every target it reached.                                                                                            |
| **Resistance roll (single target)** | The target's owner gets a resistance request: the caster's POW against the target's POW (or current magic points, as the spell says).         |
| **Resistance roll, per target**     | The same, one request per target, like Turn Undead.                                                                                           |
| **Resistance roll, whole area**     | The caster makes one roll, and the card says which POW it affects, like "affects POW 15 or lower". Every target is settled from that at once. |
| **Spirit combat**                   | The GM rules each target won or lost on the cast card, until spirit combat itself is automated.                                               |

A target may also accept the spell instead of resisting. Casting on yourself counts as accepting.

**Reflection** acts when the target's resistance holds, by roll or by the GM's ruling: if its
Reflection is strong enough (each point turns back 2 points of spirit magic or 1 Rune point), the
spell goes back at its caster, and the card says so. A stronger spell isn't reflected, and the
Reflection stays. On its way back the spell meets the caster's own protective spells, as in step 4;
if they stop it, the card reads "No effect" and nobody rolls. Otherwise the caster gets a resistance
request: their POW when they cast it against their POW now. A spell is only reflected once, even if
the caster has Reflection too.

## 6. Apply

Each target the spell takes effect on gets an **Apply** button on the card, for that target's owner.
What it does depends on the spell: it puts the spell's effect on the target, or runs the spell's
behavior, see [Spells](/rqg-system/items/spells). A spell with neither has no Apply button; the GM
and players handle the effect by hand.

On Apply, the spells already on the target have their say:

- A spell the target already has only works if the new casting is stronger, as the world setting
  **Recast Spells Take Effect** decides.
- Some spells can't share a target, and calming spells like Harmony remove weaker rage spells like
  Fanaticism, or keep them from taking hold.

## 7. While it lasts

A spell's effect shows on the token. Temporal spells last their duration.

Spells that affect a place, like Shake Earth, Warding or Sanctify, make a scene Region instead. The
caster places it, snapped to the grid, and the squares or hexes it will cover light up as it moves:

- **Ranged spells** must lie wholly within range, centered on a point the caster can see. The scene
  is dimmed outside what the caster's token can see within range. For a point the caster perceives
  by magic, the GM places it in sight and then drags the Region where it belongs.
- **Rituals** can be placed anywhere, as a ritual is held where its area is. A ward is placed by
  clicking its four wands.

The GM and the caster's players see the area; a spell like Mist Cloud is seen by everyone. The area
puts its effect on the tokens inside it and takes it off those who leave, and the GM can move or
delete it like any Region. Areas measure in meters; see
[Setting Up Scenes](/rqg-system/getting-started/scenes) for the grid settings that match them.

## 8. When it ends

A spell ends when its duration runs out, or when its effect is deleted: by Dispel or Dismiss Magic,
by the GM, or used up, like Countermagic that stopped a spell within 1 point of it. Deleting a spell
area's Region on the scene ends that spell too.

When it ends:

- **The spell's own end** runs, if it has one. A Berserker collapses helpless, and the chat says so.
- **A summoned creature** leaves: its token is removed from the scene.
- **A spell area** is removed from the scene, and everyone inside loses its effect.
- **An expired effect** is deleted from the token.

Nothing comes back when a spell ends: a weaker casting of the same spell that didn't take hold isn't
waiting underneath.

A spell runs out when the game time or the combat moves past its duration. Foundry checks that, and
the system ends the spell, on the GM's client, so it needs a GM connected.

## Who sees what

- A hidden cast (blind or private) keeps the spell's name from the target's player, even when it is
  reflected.
- Ward alarms and Sanctify alerts don't name who crossed or who cast at whom to players. The GM gets
  the names.
- Why a spell was stopped on Apply, by a spell already on the target, is only shown to that target's
  owners.
