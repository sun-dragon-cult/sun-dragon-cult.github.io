Spirit Magic Spells and Rune Magic Spells are different items, but they cast and take effect the
same way. This page explains what they have in common. The two item pages cover what is particular
to each: [Spirit Magic Spell](/rqg-system/items/spirit-magic) and
[Rune Magic Spell](/rqg-system/items/rune-magic).

## From cast to effect

1. **Cast** the spell from the actor sheet. The cast card shows the roll and the magic points or
   rune points it cost, and one row per target.
2. If the target can resist the spell, the **Resisted By** field decides how. See
   <GithubIssue issue="1066" repo="fvtt-system-rqg" />.
3. When the spell takes effect, the target's owner gets an **Apply** button on the card. What Apply
   does depends on what the spell has been given, see below.

A spell that has been given nothing still works as a cast: the card is posted and the points are
spent, but there is no Apply button and the GM and players handle the effect by hand. Spells you add
yourself start out like this.

## What a spell can be given

There are two things that make Apply do something, and a spell can have both.

**An effect template.** A spell links to an Active Effect in its **Spell Effect** field. Apply puts
a copy of that effect on the target (or on the target's weapon), named after the spell and its
level, like "Bladesharp (3)", and it shows on the token. Most spells in the wiki module work this
way. This covers everything an Active Effect can do: change the sheet, or just remind everyone that
the spell is active. The effect can also say what the spell can be cast on, and which spells can't
share a target with it.

**A behaviour.** Some spells need more than an effect on the target, like Dispel Magic, which
removes other spells, the elemental summons, which place a token, or Countermagic and Shield, which
stop other spells. These are small scripts that a module registers for the spell, which the system
runs instead of attaching an effect. The wiki module registers the behaviours for the spells it has.
Behaviours are matched on the spell's rqid, so a copy of a spell, in a world or another module, gets
the behaviour as long as it keeps the rqid.

The wiki module ships with both for the spells it covers. For any other spell, for example one from
the Red Book of Magic, see [Giving your own spells effects](/rqg-system/items/custom-spell-effects).

## Fields common to spells

| Field                                    | What it does                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Duration**, **Range**                  | From the rulebook. A Temporal duration is also what Apply gives the effect.                             |
| **Resisted By**                          | How a target can resist the spell. A single target resistance roll is automated.                        |
| **Target Kind**, **Effect Tier**         | What the spell works on and how much of it can be automated. Informational for now.                     |
| **Gets through Countermagic and Shield** | Check this for spells, like Dismiss Magic, that Countermagic and Shield don't stop.                     |
| **Spell Effect**                         | The effect template to apply. Drop an Active Effect with an rqid here, or remove it with the trash can. |

## Recasting and incompatible spells

By the rules a spell cast on a target that already has it only works if the new casting is stronger,
and some spells can't be on the same target together. The system checks this when Apply is clicked,
by the spells' rqids. The world setting **Recast Spells Take Effect** chooses between the rules as
written and always replacing. See the [6.2.0 release notes](/release-notes/rqg-v6.2.0) for details.
